# Changelog

What changed in each release of PSRemotePlayTogether. The section of a version
becomes the "What's new" part of its release notes (`scripts/ci/release-notes.sh`).

## Unreleased

- **New: play with mouse and keyboard in a web browser too.** What the app got in 0.5.1 is now on the page, and it is the same thing: **Keyboard & mouse** (before joining, and in the stream menu under Controller) is the app's settings page of that name, row for row, with the same speeds, the acceleration curve to drag, dead zone and smoothing, the two layouts, up to three keys or mouse buttons for each button of the controller, and **Try it here**. **Mouse aims** is in the stream menu and F8 catches the mouse and lets it go, as in the app; in a browser a click on the picture catches it too, and Esc always gives it back. The mouse can be the touchpad as well. The page works out exactly the same controller as the app does.
- **New in a web browser: try your microphone, speakers and webcam before joining.** **Voice & webcam** on the card for joining has the app's microphone test with the mark from which your friends hear you, the chime on the left, the right and both, and your webcam's picture as the others would get it. Nothing of it is sent.
- **Everybody sees who joined from a web browser.** The **WEB** mark on a player was only shown to the host; now the host tells everybody, so friends with the app and friends in a browser see it too (once the host has this version).
- **New: an installer for Windows.** Next to the zip there is now a `setup.exe`: it puts the app into your own programs, with an entry in the Start menu and in Windows' list of installed apps, and asks for no administrator. The app still updates itself from inside, and takes its uninstaller along when it does. The zip stays for whoever wants the app in a folder of their choice.
- **The page for web browsers can sit on the home screen like an app.** On a phone or tablet, Chrome and Edge offer **Install this page** at the foot of the page; on an iPhone or iPad it is **Share → Add to Home Screen**. It then opens full screen, without the browser's bars. This is in the page itself, so it is there without an update of the app.

## 0.5.2

- **The intro film shows its rings.** The explorer jumped for rings and over crystals that were never drawn; only the count in the corner went up. They are there now, with the sparks and the +1 when a ring is caught, the dust under a jump and the word that says what the game runs on. The film at the top of the README shows them as well.
- **A smoother picture in a web browser.** Frames reach a browser a little unevenly: two within one refresh of the screen, then none. The page showed only the newer of the two, so in an unlucky rhythm every fourth frame was never seen, which looks like a stutter however fast the connection is. Now one frame may wait for the next refresh and every frame is shown, at most 17 ms later. This is in the page itself, so it reaches everybody who joins in a browser without an update of the app.
- **When a friend's browser cannot keep up, both of you are told.** A browser's connection slows down when packets get lost, the app's does not. If the picture has to start over for a friend again and again, the host now gets a note with the friend's name and what helps (a lower bitrate under Settings → Stream quality, or the app instead of a browser), and the friend gets one on the page. Until now both only saw a picture that stuttered.
- **Settings → Stream quality** says what to try for friends in a web browser: 720p and 6 to 8 Mbit/s.

## 0.5.1

- **New: play with mouse and keyboard, as in a game on a computer.** Switch on **The mouse aims** (Settings → Keyboard & mouse, or **Mouse aims** in the stream menu): moving the mouse moves the right stick, WASD walks, the left button fires and the right one aims. The app makes an ordinary controller of it on your own computer, so it works with a PS5 or PS4, an Xbox and a game on a computer, for the host and for every friend who plays in the app. While you play, the mouse is caught: F8 lets it go and catches it again, and the stream menu lets it go too.
- **Every key and mouse button can be changed**, up to three for each button of the controller: keys (Shift, Ctrl and Alt on their own too), mouse buttons and the wheel. **Layouts** sets them all at once: Mouse and WASD, or the keys the app came with. The keys you had set are still there.
- **How the mouse aims is yours to set:** its speed left and right and up and down, an acceleration curve (even, fine, quick, or one you drag into shape yourself), how far the smallest movement pushes the stick past the game's dead zone, smoothing, a slower mouse while you aim down the sights, and up and down the other way round.
- **Try it here**, on the same page: a controller that shows what your keys and your mouse do, without a game.
- **Walking with keys as it should be:** two opposite keys cancel, and letting one go leaves the other (before, the stick stopped); diagonals go as far as a stick does; and a key for walking pushes the stick only part of the way.
- **Ctrl as a key to play with:** where Ctrl is bound to a button, holding it while pressing another key no longer closes the app or changes the picture; ⌘O or Ctrl+O still opens the stream menu.
- **On a Mac, the caught mouse is held in place the way games do it**, which needs no permission. This goes for a guest who plays the host's computer game with mouse and keyboard as well.
- **New: the app updates itself.** When a newer version is out, **Update and restart** (Settings → Updates & logs, or the question at the start) downloads it, checks it against the sum the release publishes, puts it in the app's place and starts it again: on a Mac the app where it is, on Windows the app's folder, on Linux the AppImage. Nothing is downloaded without your click, and only from this project's releases on GitHub. Where the app cannot replace itself (started straight from the disk image, a folder it may not change, a build that is no AppImage), the page says why, and **Open the download page** is still there. Versions before this one are updated by hand one last time.
- **New: what is new, in the app.** Updates & logs shows what a newer version brings before you install it, and what changed in this version and in every one before it.
- **Community and problems:** Updates & logs leads to **r/PSRemotePlayTogether** on Reddit, for questions, news and people to play with, and to **Report a problem** on GitHub.
- **Good to know:** a console game reads a stick as how fast to turn, with a dead zone and a top speed of its own. So aiming with a mouse plays well once the speeds fit the game, but not like a game made for a mouse. Some online games do not allow a mouse through a controller in their rules. In a browser the keyboard plays as before; the mouse does not aim there yet.

## 0.5.0

- **New: the intro film.** What the app is for and how it works, in under a minute: friends' controllers playing one game, what you can share, inviting with a link, who plays, voice chat and webcams, streaming. With its own music and sounds, made for it. It plays once by itself on a new installation, and **How it works** on the start page shows it again any time. Space pauses, the arrows jump from chapter to chapter, M switches the sound off, Esc leaves; a controller works too. At its end, **Host a game** and **Join a friend** lead straight on.
- **New: share a game on this computer (Windows, Linux, macOS).** On the Host page, **This computer** stands next to your consoles: pick a game's window, another window or a whole screen, and your friends join as they join a console, in the app or in a browser. PC games, Steam, emulators, GeForce Now: whatever runs in a window. The app finds which game runs (Steam, Epic, EA, Ubisoft, GOG, Battle.net, Riot and Xbox games, by the GamerNova client's detection) and the room says so.
- **Xbox through the Xbox app.** Share the Xbox app's window with Remote Play to your own Xbox, or a cloud game with Game Pass: your friends' controllers become Xbox controllers on this computer, which the Xbox app takes, and the room says Xbox.
- **Your friends' controllers become controllers on this computer.** On Windows they need the free ViGEmBus driver, installed once; the Host page says when it is missing and links it. Without it your friends can watch. Rumble from the game reaches them.
- **Linux too.** The system itself asks which window or screen to share (on Wayland and X11, through its screen sharing) and remembers it for next time. Your friends' controllers become Xbox controllers through the kernel's uinput: if the app may not add them yet, the Host page shows the one command that allows it (Steam's controller support sets up the same). Friends hear the game's own sound; without a game found, every program's but this app's and voice chats'. The picture comes from the graphics card (VAAPI for Intel and AMD, NVENC for NVIDIA).
- **And on a Mac (macOS 13 or newer).** Share a window or a screen with its sound, scaled by macOS itself; allow the app once under Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording (the Host page leads there). Your friends watch: macOS does not let apps add game controllers. Games are found by their App Store category or their Steam folder.
- **New way to play: Each their own controller.** For a game on this computer: you are player 1, your friends players 2, 3 and 4, each on a controller of their own, and their controller's lights say which. Couch co-op games become online co-op.
- **Only the game's sound.** Friends hear the shared program (a whole screen: everything but this app), never the voice chat, so nobody hears themselves twice. You hear the game as always.
- **Sharp and wide.** The picture keeps the window's shape, ultrawide too, at 720p, 1080p, 1440p or 4K (Host page → Picture), never larger than the window. The page says how much upload each friend takes. The picture comes from the graphics card's own encoder (NVIDIA, AMD, Intel or Windows' own).
- **The room stays while the console sleeps, and friends wake it.** Switch on **Room stays while it sleeps** on the Host page: when the console goes to sleep (you or a friend put it to sleep, or it did not come back), the room stays open on your computer, friends stay in or join, and **Wake the console** in the stream menu (or on the page in the browser) wakes it; everybody comes back on their own once it plays. If it does not wake up, the room stays and they can try again. For a console on your own network; your computer and the app have to stay on.
- **Mouse and keyboard together.** For games played with them: switch on **Mouse and keyboard** on the Host page, and the friends you allow (player's menu) play with their own mouse and keys, in the app or in the browser (a click on the picture catches the mouse, Esc gives it back). The mouse belongs to one player at a time, whoever moved it last; keys from everybody count together. They only reach the shared game while it is in front, and never the Windows or Command key, Alt+Tab or Alt+F4. On a Mac this needs the Accessibility permission, and it is how friends play there.
- **Player numbers.** In Each their own controller everybody sees who is which player (P1 the host, P2 to P4 the friends), in the app, on the picture and in the browser. Newcomers get the lowest free number; the host gives somebody another one in the player's menu, and whoever had it swaps.
- **Picture and sound, or the controller only, any time.** A guest switches between the two in the stream menu while playing (in the browser too), no longer only when joining.
- **Rooms say what the game runs on:** PS5, PS4, PC or Xbox, in the app and in the browser, and a **Console** filter narrows the list to one.
- **The share command:** `PSRemotePlayTogether share "<part of a window's title>"` (or `screen`) hosts a game on this computer as set up on the Host page.
- **Registering a console by its address in Settings** now always adds it to the start page; after clicking on a console found on the network first, it could stay missing.
- **Try your microphone, speakers and webcam before you play** (Settings → Voice & webcam): a level meter with the mark from which your friends hear you, a chime on the left, then the right, then both speakers, and your webcam's picture as your friends get it. Nothing is sent.
- **Setting sets: rename them, and see what is in each.** A pencil renames a set, the one in use too, and every set shows its consoles, its PSN login and streamer mode, no longer only the one in use.
- **The login PIN for any registered console**, also while it is away from your network (Settings → Your consoles → PIN).
- **Controllers say their name and charge** (Settings → Controllers): "Xbox Series X Controller" instead of "Controller", with a small battery (full, half full, low, almost empty) or "On a cable".
- **Xbox controllers over Bluetooth show their charge on Windows.** Windows' own controller interface gives none for them, so the room showed no battery; the app now reads what Windows' Bluetooth settings show.
- **The page for browsers on a phone held upright:** the picture at the top, the controller on the screen below it, every button within reach and none on top of another. Held sideways and on tablets, L3, R3 and PS no longer overlap, and the shoulder buttons stay clear of the first players' names.
- **A DualSense in the browser (Chrome, Edge):** the light bar now takes its colour over Bluetooth too, and a battery problem (too hot, too cold) no longer shows as a charge.
- **On a cable, a plug.** A controller on its cable shows a plug and "cable" where the battery was, on the game picture, in the browser and in the OBS overlay.
- **No controller, no drawing.** "Everybody's controller" leaves out players who have none connected (the keyboard and the controls on a phone's screen count as one), and the stream menu shows their controller sign faint, with "no controller connected" in their menu.
- **Settings → Permissions (Mac):** what macOS has to allow the app (devices on your network, microphone, camera, screen and system audio recording, accessibility), what each is for, and one click to allow it: macOS asks in its own window where it still asks, otherwise the click opens the right place in System Settings. **Allow all** asks for everything at once.
- **Good to know:** this version speaks a new version of the session protocol: everybody in a session needs it, older apps see the room but cannot join. A game in exclusive fullscreen or protected video can stay black: use borderless or windowed. Some anti-cheat systems refuse virtual controllers. On Linux the app needs PipeWire and the screen sharing of your desktop (xdg-desktop-portal).

## 0.4.0

- **New: go live on Twitch from the app, without OBS.** Set it up once under **Settings → Live streaming**: Twitch (or any other service that takes RTMP or RTMPS, such as YouTube or Kick), your stream key, the picture (720p or 1080p, 30 or 60 frames a second) and the bitrate. Then press **Go live** in the stream menu. The stream is what you see and hear: the game picture with the display on top of it (players, webcams, controllers, as arranged under Overlays & OBS), the game sound, the other players' voices and, if you like, your own microphone, each with its own volume. It never shows the stream menu, the chat or invites. The stream menu shows how long you are live, the upload rate and any pictures left out because the connection could not keep up; a lost connection is made again by itself.
- **Every player goes live on their own.** Host and guests each stream their own view from their own computer, straight to Twitch. Nothing passes through a server of ours.
- **Everybody sees who is live.** A red **LIVE** tag shows next to that player's name, in the app and in the browser.
- **Your choice to be left out.** **Leave me out of their streams** (Settings → Live streaming, and **In streams / Not in streams** in the browser's menu) keeps your voice, webcam and name out of what the other players stream; there you appear as "Player 2" without a picture. A player who is live sees **NOT IN STREAM** on your tile.
- **The stream key stays private.** It is kept in the macOS keychain, the Windows Credential Manager or the system keyring on Linux, never in the settings file, and it is never shown. Twitch is reached over TLS (RTMPS), and the app checks the server's certificate before it sends the key.
- **New: stream scenes.** Six full pictures for the moments that are not the game, in the look of the app: **Starting soon** with a countdown and who is in the room, **Lobby** with everybody's webcams and who is talking, **Be right back** over a blurred picture of the game, **Play with us** with the room, a live picture of the game and how to get in (a QR code to the page for browsers in a public room), **Technical difficulties** with the state of the console, the voice chat and the players, and **Thanks for watching** with the game, the time played together and everybody who played. Pick one under **Scenes** in the stream menu: it takes the place of the game picture in your live stream, and a note on your screen says what your viewers see. **Settings → Stream scenes** takes your channel, your next stream, the minutes of the countdown and the break, and every text.
- **Scenes in OBS too.** Each scene is a Browser Source of the app's overlay server (`http://localhost:9412/scene/brb`, …), filled live like the overlay; `/scene/current` follows the scene picked in the stream menu and shows nothing while the game is on. `?demo=1` shows made-up players, to set a scene up without a session.
- **Technical difficulties by itself.** While the app gets the console back after a lost connection, the stream shows Technical difficulties instead of a frozen picture, and the stream itself keeps going (before, a lost console connection would have ended it).
- **New: scene music.** Starting soon, Be right back and Thanks for watching each have music of their own: cozy loops made for the app, safe to stream, that loop without a gap. Each has a switch and a volume under **Settings → Stream scenes → Music**, and can play your own files instead (MP3, Ogg, FLAC or WAV, in order or shuffled). The music fades in and out when the scene changes, in your own stream and in OBS. If a file of yours cannot be played, the app plays its own track and tells you on that page.
- **New: log in with Twitch.** **Settings → Twitch**: you confirm a code on twitch.tv and the app never sees your password. The app then hears what happens on your channel, straight from Twitch. The login is kept in the system's password store.
- **New: Twitch alerts.** A follow, sub, resub, gift subs, cheer, raid, channel point reward, hype train, poll, prediction, goal, charity donation, shoutout or ad break shows as a card over your stream, one after the other with "N more coming up" under it. Each kind has its own card with its own emblem and colour; an alert with a message to read (a resub, a cheer, a reward with text) gets the wide card with the message. Under **Settings → Alerts** each kind can be switched off and has its own sound, volume, time on screen and minimum (bits from 100, say), plus a **Test** that you hear. **Skip**, **Clear** and **Hold** are in the stream menu, and alerts wait while Be right back is on. Viewers' names and messages are shown as plain text, cut short, with words you list hidden. In OBS: `http://localhost:9412/alerts`.
- **New: Twitch elements on the game picture.** Latest follower, sub, gift subs, bits, raid and reward; top cheerer; this stream's followers, subs and bits; viewers and uptime; your follower and sub goals, the hype train, a running poll and prediction, a charity campaign and the next ad break (only while they run); the recent events in one line; and Twitch chat. Each one has its own switch (**Settings → Twitch elements**) and its own place: drag them on the game picture (stream menu → Overlays → Arrange) or in the browser, and the place is the same in your own stream, in OBS and in the OBS export. They take the look of the other elements (no boxes, glass or dark boxes). Messages a moderator deletes disappear from the chat element too.
- **New: the whole OBS layout as one file.** **Export for OBS** (Settings → Twitch elements) saves a scene collection for OBS (Scene Collection → Import): the game with the overlay and the alerts, and a scene for each stream scene, with the alerts on every scene.
- **Rooms that are live on Twitch.** With **Show my stream in the room list** (Settings → Twitch, off by default), a public room shows **On Twitch** while your channel is live, with **Watch on Twitch** next to Join, in the app and on the page for browsers. That goes for guests too: a guest's channel reaches the host and shows in the host's room ("2 on Twitch", a **Watch** button for each). **Only rooms streaming on Twitch** narrows the list to those. The list keeps only channel names, never an address; the links to twitch.tv are made by the app and the page.
- **Sounds you control.** All alert sounds have one level and a switch on top of each kind's own; moving a slider plays the sound at its new level. A soft **chat sound** (on by default) tells you about new messages in Twitch's chat and the room's chat, never for your own and at most one every 1.5 seconds; **Also on stream** (off by default) lets your viewers hear it too. Every sound is the app's own, made for it, safe to stream.
- **Good to know:** the stream uses the computer's own H.264 encoder (VideoToolbox on a Mac; NVIDIA, AMD or Windows' own encoder on Windows; VAAPI, NVIDIA or OpenH264 on Linux). The display on the game picture goes into the stream with the Vulkan renderer (the default); with OpenGL the stream shows the game alone. Players with an older version of the app do not see the LIVE tag and cannot ask to be left out.

## 0.3.3

- **Waking the console failed with "Network error" on macOS.** After an update macOS can keep the app away from the devices in your network (Privacy & Security → Local Network): the app still finds the console, but cannot wake it or play. A firewall such as LuLu can do the same, or keep the app from finding the console at all. The app now notices both and says so on the start page, with a button to the macOS settings, and a failed wake-up says what to do instead of "Network error". The app also tells macOS why it needs your network, for the question macOS asks.
- **Fixed: the app could quit while a firewall blocked it.** A blocked send no longer ends the app.

## 0.3.2

- **Fixed: questions in the top left corner.** "Update available", "Quit?" and the other questions of the app opened squeezed into the top left corner, too small for their text, with their buttons hanging out below and the window behind them not dimmed. They now open in the middle, dimmed behind. The same fix puts the About window (Settings → Updates & logs) and the window of a controller (Settings → Controllers) in the middle.

## 0.3.1

- **Clearer choices for who gets in.** A public room now offers **Ask to join** (you approve each player first), **Join as viewers** (they play once you allow it) and **Join and play** (no approval, they play right away), each with a line that says what it means. The stream menu and the list of public rooms use the same words, so a room in the list shows *Ask to join*, *Join as viewer* or *Join and play*, and the filter is **Hide rooms that ask to join**. Before, "Everybody, playing at once" meant "right away" while **All at once** under Who controls meant "at the same time", and "Open" sat oddly next to **Open to newcomers**.
- **Who controls says what it does.** The choices are **Together**, **One at a time** and **Only me**, with a line under them on the Host page and in the stream menu: everybody's buttons count at the same time, whoever moves takes over while the others rest, or your friends watch. The list says *Everybody plays together*.
- **"Only me" shows everybody else as watching.** While only the host controls, the other players show as watching in the stream menu, on the game picture, in the OBS overlay and in the browser, with "only the host plays" in their menu. **Let them play** is dimmed until the host lets others control again, and the Host page says that everybody watches either way.

## 0.3.0

- **A new look.** The whole app is redesigned: the start page with its two doors, **Host** and **Join**, the Host and Join pages, the stream menu, the settings and every dialog. In the game, Play Together sits in a panel at the side (who knocks, who controls, the players with their webcams, your voice and webcam, the chat), with sound, picture, quality and overlays in a bar at the bottom. Everything works with a controller as well as with a mouse. The page for browsers looks the same.
- **One settings window.** The settings and the Play Together settings are one window with a sidebar (Playing, Play Together, Consoles, App) and your profile on top. A search field finds any setting on any page and lights it up there. Every setting is one row in plain words; choices are buttons you see at once, on and off are switches, amounts are sliders with their value. Keys are changed right on the page: click a key, press the new one. The meeting point and public rooms have real switches, and an address of your own comes back when you switch them on again. Each console has its own **Start right away** switch (was auto-connect). "Profiles" are now **Setting sets**, so they are not mixed up with your player profile. **Your profile** shows how the others see you.
- **Fixed: "Copy the address"** next to the renderer's documentation copied the PSN login address instead.
- **Fixed: no note for new chat messages.** While the chat was closed, a message from somebody else never showed on the game picture in the app, although the option for it was on. It does now, for a few seconds at the bottom left, as it already did in the browser.
- **Guests are no longer asked about the console's sleep.** Leaving a session asked every guest whether the console should go to sleep, and the answer did nothing: only the host can do that. Guests are not asked any more.
- **New: guests may put the console to sleep, if you say so.** **Guests may put the console to sleep when they leave** on the Host page, off by default. With it on, a guest who leaves is asked, in the app and in the browser, and "Sleep" ends the session for everybody. Waking the console stays the host's: a session only exists once the host's app is connected.
- **New: Host… and Join….** The start page has two buttons instead of forms spread over several pages. **Host…** sets the session up before it starts: private or public, how many friends, who controls, browsers, the console's sleep, "I play on the TV", and the invites to send. **Join…** takes an invite, or a room from the list of public rooms. **Play** on the console is for playing on your own; the app remembers which of the two you chose last, for when it connects at start.
- **New: public rooms.** Choose **Public** on the Host page, give the room a title and a language, and it is listed for everybody while it runs, with the game on the console and its picture, how many play, who controls (everybody at once, one at a time, only the host) and whether browsers are welcome. **Join… → Public rooms** searches the list by title, game, host and language, filters by language, full rooms and rooms that ask first, and sorts it. The page for browsers has the same list. The picture comes from Sony's public catalog, which the host's app asks about the game, without a login. The list only says how to find a room: joining goes straight to the host, and a public room has a key of its own, new for every public session. As with every invite, players connect to the host's computer directly and so learn its internet address; for a public room that can be anybody who reads the list.
- **New: a live picture in the list.** A public room shows what is being played right now: every 45 seconds the host's app takes one frame of the stream, makes a small picture of it (320×180) and sends it to the list, which shows it with a "Live" mark instead of the cover, in the app and on the page. **Live picture in the list** on the Host page switches it off; in streamer mode it is always off.
- **New: who plays, in the list.** Each public room shows its players as small circles, the host first: their own picture where they chose one, otherwise the first letter of their name, with how many play of how many. Only initials and pictures go to the list, never the other players' names.
- **New: you decide who gets in.** A public room lets each player in when the host says so, lets newcomers watch until the host lets them play, or is open to everybody. Who knocks shows as a note on the game picture and in the stream menu, with **Let in** and **No**; a guest at the door sees "Waiting for the host to let you in…". **Closed to newcomers** in the stream menu takes the room off the list and turns newcomers away, while players who were in before can come back. **Remove and block** keeps a player out for the rest of the session. A player keeps whether they may control when they come back.
- **One place for each thing.** Nothing to join or share is on two pages any more, and the settings only hold settings. Menus and lists open where they fit, never cut off at the edge of the window.
- **For servers of your own:** the list of public rooms is one small PHP file, like the meeting point (`server/rooms` in the source code). **Settings → Network & invites → Servers** takes its address, or switches public rooms off.
- **In the browser:** when a controller goes away on a phone or tablet, the controls on the screen come back.

## 0.2.0

- **Join from a web browser.** A friend without the app opens a link in Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari, plugs in a controller and plays along: same picture and sound, voice chat, text chat with link previews and emoji, webcams (their own too), a player picture, rumble. Nothing to install, no account. Without a controller the keyboard stands in, and phones and tablets get controls on the screen.
- **The browser looks and works like the app.** The same display on the game picture (players, webcams, everybody's controller, the console's controller, with the same presets and the same arranging by dragging), the same stream menu (Ctrl+O or ⌘O, L1+R1+L3+R3, or the Menu button), the same chat and the same options. The new **Web browser** invite is on the start page, under Play Together and in the stream menu.
- **Still no server in between.** The browser connects to the host's computer directly (WebRTC). The page only finds the host through the meeting point, the same way the app does without a forwarded port. The session's key stands behind the `#` of the link, which a browser sends to nobody, and the page takes it out of the address bar.
- **Browser guests are guests like the others.** They show in the player list with a small "WEB" mark and can be muted, set to watching and removed. The page comes back by itself after a lost connection.
- **Yours to switch off or host.** **Play Together → Friends can join from a web browser** switches it off. The page is a folder of static files (`web/`); **Page for browsers** takes the address of your own copy.
- **Good to know:** in a browser the game's haptics arrive as plain rumble, and it adds a little delay. Touchpad, motion sensors, adaptive triggers and the light bar need a DualSense that the browser opens directly (Chrome and Edge, "DualSense…" in the stream menu); that part is new and little tested. A PS5 streams H.265, which Chrome, Edge and Safari play on most computers; otherwise the host can switch to H.264. With most routers a browser gets in by itself, like the app. If one cannot, the host forwards **UDP port 9411** as well: browsers come in on a port of their own, one above the sharing port.
- **For meeting points of your own:** a web page can only use one that answers with `Access-Control-Allow-Origin`. The script can send it now (`PSRPT_MEET_CORS`), and its self-test says whether a browser could use it.
- **Fixed: a console that refuses was asked a hundred times a second.** When the first connection to a console failed (already in use, not awake yet), the app tried again every few milliseconds for 20 seconds. It now waits a second between tries, and cancelling stops them at once.
- **Fixed: `join` on the command line could end up at your own console.** With "connect at start" set and the console awake, a start that was meant to join a friend's session connected to the console first and the join was refused.
- **Fixed: controller colours on the game picture.** With a colour other than white, black or red, the controllers on the game picture had no sticks and showed no pressed shoulder buttons, directions or touchpad.

## 0.1.3

- **Join without a forwarded port.** With most routers the host no longer has to open a port. Host and guest find each other through a small meeting point on the internet and then connect directly ("hole punching"). Picture, sound and controllers never pass through a server. A forwarded port keeps working as before, and it is used by itself when it is there.
- **The host is told what to expect.** While you share, the stream menu says whether friends can get in without a forwarded port, or that your router is of the kind that still needs one (symmetric NAT).
- **Clearer messages when joining fails.** A guest is told whether the host was not found at all, or was found but the two routers did not let the connection through.
- **Invites survive a new address.** An Internet invite whose address is out of date, because the host's connection got a new one over night, still leads to the host through the meeting point.
- **Your own meeting point.** It is one small PHP file (`server/meeting-point` in the source code). Play Together has a field for its address, and "off" switches the meeting point off altogether.
- **IPv6.** A session now sends from the same IPv6 address that the IPv6 invite names, so a firewall rule made for that address also fits the answers.
- **Good to know:** hole punching does not get through every router. Mobile networks and some fibre connections still need the forwarded port or a mesh VPN.
